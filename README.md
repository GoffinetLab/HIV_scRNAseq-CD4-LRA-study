# HIV_CD4_LRA_study

## Abstract

Shock-and-kill is a leading HIV-1 cure strategy, but its potential remains unrealized due to limited mechanistic understanding. Histone deacetylase inhibitors (HDACis) reactivate HIV-1 transcription in vitro, ex vivo and in vivo, yet fail to reduce the viral reservoir in people living with HIV-1. Using single-cell RNA-sequencing and functional assays on CD4+ T-cells from aviremic donors, we show that clinically relevant panobinostat concentrations broadly suppress T-cell activation and interferon-driven antiviral programmes across most CD4+ T-cell subsets, rendering cells refractory to stimulation. Exogenous type I interferon failed to overcome this block or affect HIV-1 reactivation itself, but instead imposed a pre-budding restriction and increased surface HIV-1 Env on reactivating cells, rendering them more susceptible to NK cell-mediated antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, most pronounced with IFN-β and -α14. These findings provide proof-of-concept that combining potent latency-reversing agents with type I interferons can overcome the resistance of HIV-1-reactivating T-cells to immune elimination, informing improved shock-and-kill strategies.
